From Barely 400 Meters to My First 10K: A Story of Consistency

There was a time when running 400 meters felt like punishment to me. I still remember stopping midway, breathing heavily, pretending to “walk for recovery,” while others continued running effortlessly around the track. Back then, I genuinely believed long-distance running was only for naturally athletic people.

And honestly, if someone had told me that one day I would complete a 10-kilometer run, I probably would have laughed.

But yesterday, on the athletics track at IIT Madras and across the campus, after running 10.43 kilometers in 1 hour and 24 minutes, I realized something important:

MOST LIMITS ARE TEMPORARY. CONSISTENCY IS NOT.

The Invisible Journey

This run was not just about fitness. It was about proving to myself that progress is possible even when the starting point feels weak. What makes this journey meaningful is not the final statistic on Strava. It is the invisible journey behind it:

  • the days I wanted to quit,
  • the laps where I slowed down,
  • the mornings I showed up without motivation,
  • the small improvements nobody noticed except me.

There was no dramatic transformation overnight. No “one secret trick.” Just repeated effort. And somewhere between those difficult first 400 meters and this 10K finish line, I understood a lesson that applies far beyond running.

Growth is Painfully Invisible

Whether it is:

  • building technical skills,
  • learning artificial intelligence,
  • working on research,
  • creating startups,
  • improving yourself physically and mentally,

the process is always the same: you struggle first, you improve slowly, and one day you look back and realize you became someone your older self would admire.

As an engineering student, I have started appreciating endurance differently. In academics and projects, people often celebrate intelligence, but very few talk about consistency. The truth is, difficult goals are usually achieved not by sudden motivation, but by sustained discipline. Running taught me that.

“A 10K run is essentially a long conversation with yourself.”

At some point, your body gets tired and your mind starts negotiating:
  • “Stop now”
  • “Slow down”
  • “You’ve already done enough”

And every extra lap becomes less physical and more mental.

Reflecting on Life

That mindset has started reflecting in other parts of my life too:

  • sitting longer with difficult problems,
  • staying patient while learning,
  • handling failures better,
  • and understanding that meaningful growth takes time.

Completing this run may not look extraordinary to the world. There are people who run faster, farther and better every single day. But for me, this run represents something deeply personal: proof that I can outgrow my own limitations.

The person who once struggled to complete 400 meters could never imagine this moment.

And maybe that is the best part of growth: your future self keeps surprising your present self.

This is only the beginning.
